\begin{acknowledgements}


There are always too many people to thank; the below is a non-exhaustive summary of people without whom it would not have been possible:

Firstly, the members of the Schultz lab. To Christina Thompson and Aled David's, whose indefatigable efforts meant that the monkeys received infinitely more value outside the task than within. And to Drs. Philipe Bujold, Alexandre Pastor-Bernier, Fabian Grabenhorst, Simone Ferrari-Toniolo, and Konstantin Volkmann, whose discussions and guidance made me a better researcher. Finally, to Aileen Jordan and my advisor Steve Edgley, who dealt with my anxieties and the outrageous delays to submitting this thesis. My examiners, Prof. Vincent Mak and Prof. Elliot Ludvig, also deserve thanks for their thorough and constructive engagement with this thesis.

Thanks also to Martin Crevier and the Darwin College Boat Club for somewhere productive to exhaust my scientific frustrations. Outside of Cambridge, thanks to all who had the grace not to ask how the thesis was going, chiefly Eleanor Wilson (and the rest of the GPITW), Akhil Nair, Joaquim D'Souza (and the Latymer group), Rory Ellwood (and the Alpacas), Tom Ayers, and John Stowers (and the Vienna Biocenter cohort). There are many more.

It is also worth acknowledging those who made this thesis more difficult. To Dr. David Keays and Dr. Kristin Teßmar, as it happens, I am neither too lazy nor too stupid to be a scientist.

Above all I would like to thank Prof. Wolfram Schultz, who gave me a second chance when few others would; Dr. Daniel Hill, the most committed scientist I'll ever meet; my parents, who never indulged my excuses not to finish this thesis; and my sisters, whose relentless accomplishments provide an inescapable achievement treadmill.

Finally, my life over the past 8 years can be bisected by two pairs of animals: Ash and Lily, the most loving cats I have encountered, and the monkeys whose work fills this thesis, Ulysses and Vicer. None of this would have been possible without their sacrifice.
