RPE / CAUSAL LEARNING — WRITING PLAN (Category F + G, combined)
==================================================================

Our position (agreed 2026-08-02): dopamine firing is directly proportional to /
an encoding of economic value to the animal. This is framed as a REFINEMENT of
RPE (the existing Schultz-lab utility tradition: Stauffer 2014, Lak 2014,
already cited in Ch1; reinforced by own-lab's concurrent Ferrari-Toniolo
Cell Reports paper, already cited in Ch5), not a rejection of it. Causal
learning (ANCCR) is treated as answering a DIFFERENT question (which events
get credited as causes) rather than a rival account of value-magnitude coding.
Our task varied subjective value while holding causal structure fixed, so we
can state this scope honestly rather than claiming to adjudicate ANCCR.

  studying dopaminergic signalling. If dopamine neurons implement a
  distributed code in which individual cells vary in their response
  properties and computational roles, then standard approaches that average
  responses across trials may inadvertently flatten this heterogeneity.
  Rather than every neuron performing the canonical reward prediction error
  (RPE) computation identically on each trial, individual neurons may
  contribute distinct components to the overall population signal.
  Trial-by-trial analysis of single neurons, rather than trial-averaged
  population responses, may therefore be necessary to uncover the true
  computational diversity within the dopaminergic system and understand how
  heterogeneous individual signals combine to produce reliable
  population-level representations of reward value."

---- PARAGRAPH 1 — own the tension, ground it in the field ----
Claims to make:
- OPENING MOVE (new): draw a distinction between two different questions —
  HOW dopamine encodes information (the population-/distributional-code
  question — already covered in 5.4.2 via Dabney, Lowet, Sousa) versus WHAT
  it is encoding (the semantic content: value? causal contingency?
  state-belief? — the question this subsection, and its title, is actually
  about). Not verbatim, just the guiding idea: "Dabney et al. and related
  work in 5.4.2 concern how dopamine encodes information, not what it is
  encoding." This sets 5.4.2's material aside cleanly rather than re-treading
  it, and gives the subsection's title a direct answer to build toward.
- Restate plainly (this is the "restate extinguish" beat): classical RPE
  predicts a response should extinguish once an outcome is fully predicted
- Explicitly reference Section 5.3.1's own result (chapter5.tex:83): bid-,
  profit-, and liquid-received-locked responses persisted despite monkeys
  having "perfect information" about forthcoming payment; name the existing
  "trial heterogeneity" explanation and flag it as incomplete, motivating
  what follows
- Establish this is a recognised, general phenomenon in the field, not a
  quirk of this dataset or a weakness of the existing explanation alone
Papers:
- Howe et al. (2013), Nature — seminal ramping-to-predicted-reward result
- Hamid et al. (2016), Nat Neurosci — value-of-work tracking despite
  prediction
- Sharpe, Chang, Liu, Batchelor, Mueller, Jones, Niv & Schoenbaum (2017),
  Nat Neurosci — dopamine transients sufficient/necessary for model-based
  associations, i.e. doing more than canonical RPE

---- PARAGRAPH 2 — state the position, resolve the tension ----
Claims to make:
- Position: dopamine's response tracks SUBJECTIVE utility, not objective
  predictability of physical magnitude — "perfect information about
  payment" (line 83) is not the same as "no remaining value-relevant
  variance," because bid (subjective value) still fluctuates trial-to-trial
- Callback (not new citations, already in Ch1): Stauffer & Glimcher (2014),
  Lak et al. (2014) — the existing utility-RPE lineage this thesis already
  sits inside
- New: Ferrari-Toniolo, Seak & Schultz (2025/2026) — own lab's concurrent,
  independent Cell Reports result (utility + weighted probability
  integration in the dopamine population signal) — converging evidence,
  same lab, separate analysis
- Gershman (2024), Nat Neurosci — even RPE's own defenders now reframe
  "prediction error" around richer state/value representations (ramping,
  sensory/motor features, action selection) rather than a fixed naive
  target — supports treating our account as a refinement, not a rejection
Net effect: reframes line 83's "hasn't extinguished yet" into "was never
expected to fully extinguish."

---- PARAGRAPH 3 — introduce causal learning / ANCCR properly ----
Claims to make:
- Explain what ANCCR actually claims: retrospective, causal-contingency-
  based credit assignment — a claim about WHICH events get credited as
  causes, not about how much value an event carries
- Treat it as a genuine, serious alternative worth real engagement (this is
  the examiner-named alternative — needs more than a citation-drop)
Papers:
- Jeong, Taylor, Floeder et al. (2022), Science — foundational ANCCR theory
  and evidence
- Namboodiri (2024), Curr Opin Behav Sci — concise statement of the "why"
- Garr, Cheng, Jeong, Brooke, Castell, Bal, Magnard, Namboodiri & Janak
  (2024), Science Advances — strongest single citation: direct within-paper
  model comparison where TDRL fails and ANCCR succeeds (contingency
  degradation, dopamine unchanged in timing but selectively reduced when
  statistical contingency — not temporal contiguity — is degraded)

---- PARAGRAPH 4 — position our findings relative to ANCCR; close ----
Claims to make:
- Honest scope claim: our task varied subjective value trial-to-trial while
  holding causal structure/contingency fixed throughout (cue-outcome link
  never degraded or ambiguous) — so our data speak directly to the
  value-magnitude question and are largely silent on the causal-contingency
  question ANCCR targets
- Note the two accounts are not necessarily mutually exclusive — gesture
  (briefly, as a future-work note, not a claim) toward value-weighted
  causal credit assignment as a possible synthesis
- Brief, explicitly scope-limited nod to other newer accounts so "other
  newer developments" is covered without diluting the argument:
    - Greenstreet, Martinez Vergara et al. (2025), Nature — "value-free"
      action-prediction-error signal; different pathway (tail of striatum)
      and species (mouse) — doesn't directly compete with our primate
      VTA/ventral-striatum value result
    - Friedman et al. (2025) — information-gain framing, similarly a
      different proposed signal, noted not adjudicated
    - Starkweather, Babayan, Uchida & Gershman (2017) — hidden-state
      inference framing, already named in the new Ch1 paragraph as one of
      only two alternatives flagged there; needs at least a clause here so
      the Ch1 forward-pointer isn't a dropped thread
    - Forward-reference (do not re-explain) the distributional-coding /
      heterogeneity discussion already given in 5.4.2 (Dabney 2020, Lowet
      2024, Ferrari-Toniolo) as another axis of "beyond simple scalar RPE"
- Close on synthesis: our findings most directly extend the utility-coding
  tradition; how value-based and causal-structure-based accounts of
  dopamine relate remains an open question this work raises rather than
  resolves
